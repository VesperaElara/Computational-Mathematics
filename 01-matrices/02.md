# Gaussian Elimination & Echelon Forms: Practical Reduction Techniques

## High-Level Overview

In our previous session, we defined what a **Row Echelon Form (REF)** and a **Reduced Row Echelon Form (RREF)** look like. Now, we put those definitions into practice using **Gaussian Elimination** and **Gauss-Jordan Elimination**.

* **Gaussian Elimination** is the systematic process of applying elementary row operations to reduce any given matrix into **Row Echelon Form (REF)**.
* **Gauss-Jordan Elimination** takes it a step further, pushing the matrix all the way into **Reduced Row Echelon Form (RREF)**.

An essential theorem governing these techniques states: **"Each matrix is row equivalent to one and only one reduced echelon matrix."** No matter what valid sequence of row operations you choose, you will always arrive at the exact same RREF!

## Step-by-Step Problem 1: Reducing a $4 \times 4$ Matrix to REF

Let's walk through the manual reduction of matrix $A$ into row echelon form.

### Given Matrix:
$$A = \begin{bmatrix} 
2 & 4 & 3 & -1 \\ 
0 & 1 & 8 & 3 \\ 
4 & 1 & 3 & 4 \\ 
3 & 2 & 1 & 5 
\end{bmatrix}$$

### Step 1: Create a Leading Pivot of $1$ in Row 1
To make calculations cleaner, we scale the first row by dividing by $2$:
* Operation: $R_1 \to \frac{R_1}{2}$

$$\sim \begin{bmatrix} 
1 & 2 & 3/2 & -1/2 \\ 
0 & 1 & 8 & 3 \\ 
4 & 1 & 3 & 4 \\ 
3 & 2 & 1 & 5 
\end{bmatrix}$$

### Step 2: Eliminate Entries Below the First Pivot
We want zeros in the first column under the top-left $1$. 
* Operations: $R_3 \to R_3 - 4R_1$ and $R_4 \to R_4 - 3R_1$

$$\sim \begin{bmatrix} 
1 & 2 & 3/2 & -1/2 \\ 
0 & 1 & 8 & 3 \\ 
0 & -7 & -3 & 6 \\ 
0 & -4 & -7/2 & 13/2 
\end{bmatrix}$$

### Step 3: Clear Column 2 Under the Second Pivot
Our second pivot is already $1$ in row 2 ($R_{22}$). Now, eliminate the entries below it using $R_2$:
* Operations: $R_3 \to R_3 + 7R_2$ and $R_4 \to R_4 + 4R_2$

$$\sim \begin{bmatrix} 
1 & 2 & 3/2 & -1/2 \\ 
0 & 1 & 8 & 3 \\ 
0 & 0 & 53 & 27 \\ 
0 & 0 & 57/2 & 37/2 
\end{bmatrix}$$

### Step 4: Normalize and Complete REF
Scale row 3 to get a leading entry of $1$, then clear the entry below it:
* Operations: $R_3 \to \frac{R_3}{53}$, followed by $R_4 \to R_4 - \frac{57}{2}R_3$

$$\sim \begin{bmatrix} 
1 & 2 & 3/2 & -1/2 \\ 
0 & 1 & 8 & 3 \\ 
0 & 0 & 1 & 27/53 \\ 
0 & 0 & 0 & 3.98 
\end{bmatrix}$$

This final structure satisfies all REF properties: all non-zero rows form a clear staircase pattern with zeros strictly beneath each leading pivot.

## Step-by-Step Problem 2: Reducing a Rectangular Matrix

Let's find the row echelon form of a $3 \times 6$ matrix $B$:

$$B = \begin{bmatrix} 
4 & 8 & 0 & 0 & -4 & -20 \\ 
-3 & -5 & 0 & 4 & 6 & 12 \\ 
2 & 6 & 0 & 9 & 5 & -14 
\end{bmatrix}$$

### Step 1: Scale Row 1
Make the leading coefficient $1$:
* Operation: $R_1 \to \frac{R_1}{4}$

$$\sim \begin{bmatrix} 
1 & 2 & 0 & 0 & -1 & -5 \\ 
-3 & -5 & 0 & 4 & 6 & 12 \\ 
2 & 6 & 0 & 9 & 5 & -14 
\end{bmatrix}$$

### Step 2: Clear First Column
Eliminate entries below the first pivot:
* Operations: $R_2 \to R_2 + 3R_1$ and $R_3 \to R_3 - 2R_1$

$$\sim \begin{bmatrix} 
1 & 2 & 0 & 0 & -1 & -5 \\ 
0 & 1 & 0 & 4 & 3 & -3 \\ 
0 & 2 & 0 & 9 & 7 & -4 
\end{bmatrix}$$

### Step 3: Finalize Staircase Form
Clear out the entry under the second pivot:
* Operation: $R_3 \to R_3 - 2R_2$

$$\sim \begin{bmatrix} 
1 & 2 & 0 & 0 & -1 & -5 \\ 
0 & 1 & 0 & 4 & 3 & -3 \\ 
0 & 0 & 0 & 1 & 1 & 2 
\end{bmatrix}$$

This matrix is now successfully in **row-echelon form**.

## Column Transformations and CEF

Symmetrically, matrices can be reduced into **Column Echelon Form (CEF)** or **Column Reduced Echelon Form (CREF)** using column operations (denoted by $C_i$). 

For instance, given matrix $C$, applying targeted column shifts like $C_2 \to C_2 - 2C_1$, swapping columns, and isolating pivot vectors relocates zero columns to the right and establishes a right-moving column hierarchy:

$$C = \begin{bmatrix} 
4 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 
-3 & 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 
2 & 2 & 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 
\end{bmatrix}$$
