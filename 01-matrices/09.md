# Gauss Elimination Method

---

## High-Level Overview

The **Gauss Elimination Method** is a direct numerical technique used to solve systems of linear equations. It works by transforming the augmented matrix of a system into an **upper triangular form** (or row echelon form) using elementary row operations, after which the unknowns are easily determined via **back substitution**.

---

## 1. Principles and Workflow

1. **Direct Method:** Unlike iterative methods, it solves the system in a finite number of precise steps.
2. **Pivoting:** The leading diagonal elements are chosen as **pivot elements** (e.g., $a_1$, $b_2'$, $c_3''$). 
   * **Note on Leading Entries:** There is no strict restriction that the leading entry of a row must be $1$ during the elimination process.
3. **Failure & Modification:** The method fails if any pivot element vanishes ($0$). This can typically be resolved by rearranging (swapping) the rows. If no non-zero pivot can be found by swapping, the matrix is singular and the system has no unique/standard solution.

---

## 2. Step-by-Step Procedure for a $3 \times 3$ System

Consider a general system of three linear equations:
$$
\begin{aligned}
a_1x + b_1y + c_1z &= d_1 \\
a_2x + b_2y + c_2z &= d_2 \\
a_3x + b_3y + c_3z &= d_3
\end{aligned}
$$

### Step I: Construct the Augmented Matrix
Write the system in matrix form $[A \mid B]$:
$$[A \mid B] = \begin{bmatrix} a_1 & b_1 & c_1 & \mid & d_1 \\ a_2 & b_2 & c_2 & \mid & d_2 \\ a_3 & b_3 & c_3 & \mid & d_3 \end{bmatrix}$$

### Step II: Eliminate $x$ from Lower Rows
Assuming $a_1 \neq 0$, eliminate $x$ from the second and third rows using row operations:
* $R_2 \to R_2 - \left(\frac{a_2}{a_1}\right)R_1$
* $R_3 \to R_3 - \left(\frac{a_3}{a_1}\right)R_1$

This yields an intermediate matrix where the first column has zeros below the main diagonal:
$$\begin{bmatrix} a_1 & b_1 & c_1 & \mid & d_1 \\ 0 & b_2' & c_2' & \mid & d_2' \\ 0 & b_3' & c_3' & \mid & d_3' \end{bmatrix}$$

### Step III: Eliminate $y$ from the Third Row
Using the new pivot element $b_2'$ in the second row, eliminate $y$ from the third row:
* $R_3 \to R_3 - \left(\frac{b_3'}{b_2'}\right)R_2$

This reduces the coefficient matrix to **upper triangular form**:
$$\begin{bmatrix} a_1 & b_1 & c_1 & \mid & d_1 \\ 0 & b_2' & c_2' & \mid & d_2' \\ 0 & 0 & c_3'' & \mid & d_3'' \end{bmatrix}$$

### Step IV: Back Substitution
Translate the final upper triangular matrix back into equations:
1. $c_3''z = d_3'' \implies \mathbf{z = \frac{d_3''}{c_3''}}$
2. Substitute $z$ into the second equation to solve for $\mathbf{y}$.
3. Substitute $y$ and $z$ into the first equation to solve for $\mathbf{x}$.

## 3. Solved Examples

### Example 1: Unique Solution ($3 \times 3$)
**Problem:** Solve by Gauss elimination:
$$3x - 4y + 5z = -1, \quad -3x + 2y + z = 1, \quad 6x + 8y - z = 35$$

#### Solution:
1. **Augmented Matrix:**
   $$[A \mid B] = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & -4 & 5 & \mid & -1 \\ -3 & 2 & 1 & \mid & 1 \\ 6 & 8 & -1 & \mid & 35 \end{bmatrix}$$
2. **Row Operations:**
   * Apply $R_2 \to R_2 + R_1$ and $R_3 \to R_3 - 2R_1$:
     $$\sim \begin{bmatrix} 3 & -4 & 5 & \mid & -1 \\ 0 & -2 & 6 & \mid & 0 \\ 0 & 16 & -11 & \mid & 37 \end{bmatrix}$$
   * Apply $R_3 \to R_3 + 8R_2$:
     $$\sim \begin{bmatrix} 3 & -4 & 5 & \mid & -1 \\ 0 & -2 & 6 & \mid & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 37 & \mid & 37 \end{bmatrix}$$
3. **Back Substitution:**
   * $37z = 37 \implies \mathbf{z = 1}$
   * $-2y + 6(1) = 0 \implies -2y = -6 \implies \mathbf{y = 3}$
   * $3x - 4(3) + 5(1) = -1 \implies 3x - 7 = -1 \implies 3x = 6 \implies \mathbf{x = 2}$

**Final Answer:** $x = 2, \, y = 3, \, z = 1$.

### Example 2: Infinitely Many Solutions
**Problem:** Test for consistency and solve:
$$x - z = 2, \quad y - z = -2, \quad x + y - 2z = 0$$

#### Solution:
1. **Augmented Matrix & Row Reductions:**
   $$[A \mid B] = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & -1 & \mid & 2 \\ 0 & 1 & -1 & \mid & -2 \\ 1 & 1 & -2 & \mid & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$
   * Apply $R_3 \to R_3 - R_1$, then $R_3 \to R_3 - R_2$:
     $$\sim \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & -1 & \mid & 2 \\ 0 & 1 & -1 & \mid & -2 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & \mid & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$
2. **Rank Analysis:** 
   * $\text{rank}(A) = \text{rank}(A \mid B) = 2$, which is less than the number of unknowns ($n = 3$). 
   * The system is **consistent and has infinitely many solutions**.
3. **Parameter Assignment:**
   * Since $n - r = 3 - 2 = 1$, we can assign an arbitrary value to one variable. Let $z = 0$.
   * $y - 0 = -2 \implies \mathbf{y = -2}$
   * $x - 0 = 2 \implies \mathbf{x = 2}$

**Final Answer:** One particular solution is $(2, -2, 0)$.

### Example 3: Systems with Four Unknowns ($4 \times 4$)
**Problem:** Solve the system:
$$
\begin{aligned}
5x + y + z + w &= 4 \\
x + 7y + z + w &= 12 \\
x + y + 6z + w &= -5 \\
x + y + z + 4w &= -6
\end{aligned}
$$

#### Solution:
1. **Rearrange and Form Augmented Matrix:** Swap $R_1$ and $R_4$ to place a leading $1$ at the top left for smoother calculation:
   $$[A \mid B] = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 & 4 & \mid & -6 \\ 1 & 7 & 1 & 1 & \mid & 12 \\ 1 & 1 & 6 & 1 & \mid & -5 \\ 5 & 1 & 1 & 1 & \mid & 4 \end{bmatrix}$$
2. **Perform Row Operations:**
   * $R_2 \to R_2 - R_1$, $R_3 \to R_3 - R_1$, $R_4 \to R_4 - 5R_1$:
     $$\sim \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 & 4 & \mid & -6 \\ 0 & 6 & 0 & -3 & \mid & 18 \\ 0 & 0 & 5 & -3 & \mid & 1 \\ 0 & -4 & -4 & -19 & \mid & 34 \end{bmatrix}$$
   * Further elimination steps yield the upper triangular form:
     $$\sim \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 & 4 & \mid & -6 \\ 0 & 6 & 0 & -3 & \mid & 18 \\ 0 & 0 & 5 & -3 & \mid & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & -117 & \mid & 234 \end{bmatrix}$$
3. **Back Substitution:**
   * $-117w = 234 \implies \mathbf{w = -2}$
   * $5z - 3(-2) = 1 \implies 5z + 6 = 1 \implies 5z = -5 \implies \mathbf{z = -1}$
   * $6y - 3(-2) = 18 \implies 6y + 6 = 18 \implies 6y = 12 \implies \mathbf{y = 2}$
   * $x + (2) + (-1) + 4(-2) = -6 \implies x - 7 = -6 \implies \mathbf{x = 1}$

**Final Answer:** $(x, y, z, w) = (1, 2, -1, -2)$.
