# Computing Matrix Rank Using Echelon Form

## High-Level Overview

We have learned how to reduce a matrix into **Row Echelon Form (REF)** using elementary operations. Now, we connect this technique directly to the **rank of a matrix**.

### The Core Principle
Instead of calculating complex determinants of minors, engineering mathematics allows us to define the **row rank** as the exact number of non-zero rows present in the matrix's row echelon form. 

* **Theorem:** The row rank of a matrix is always equal to its column rank, and this common value is called simply the **rank of the matrix** ($\rho(A)$). 
* **Linear Independence:** Conceptually, the rank tells us the **maximum number of linearly independent row or column vectors** inside that matrix.
* **Important Bounds:** For any $m \times n$ matrix $A$, the rank is bounded by the smaller dimension: $\rho(A) \le \min(m, n)$.

## Step-by-Step Worked Example 1: Finding Rank via REF

Let's compute the rank of matrix $A$ by transforming it into row echelon form.

### Given Matrix:
$$A = \begin{bmatrix} 
2 & -1 & 3 \\ 
1 & 0 & 1 \\ 
0 & 2 & -1 \\ 
1 & 1 & 4 
\end{bmatrix}$$

### Step 1: Swap Rows to Get a Clean Pivot
Swap row 1 and row 2 to place a $1$ at the top-left position:
* Operation: $R_1 \leftrightarrow R_2$

$$\sim \begin{bmatrix} 
1 & 0 & 1 \\ 
2 & -1 & 3 \\ 
0 & 2 & -1 \\ 
1 & 1 & 4 
\end{bmatrix}$$

### Step 2: Clear Out Column 1 Under the Pivot
Perform row operations to eliminate entries below the leading $1$:
* Operations: $R_2 \to R_2 - 2R_1$ and $R_4 \to R_4 - R_1$

$$\sim \begin{bmatrix} 
1 & 0 & 1 \\ 
0 & -1 & 1 \\ 
0 & 2 & -1 \\ 
0 & 1 & 3 
\end{bmatrix}$$

### Step 3: Normalize the Second Row Pivot
Scale the second row to make the leading entry positive:
* Operation: $R_2 \to (-1)R_2$

$$\sim \begin{bmatrix} 
1 & 0 & 1 \\ 
0 & 1 & -1 \\ 
0 & 2 & -1 \\ 
0 & 1 & 3 
\end{bmatrix}$$

### Step 4: Continue Down the Staircase
Clear entries below the second row pivot:
* Operations: $R_3 \to R_3 - 2R_2$ and $R_4 \to R_4 - R_2$

$$\sim \begin{bmatrix} 
1 & 0 & 1 \\ 
0 & 1 & -1 \\ 
0 & 0 & 1 \\ 
0 & 0 & 4 
\end{bmatrix}$$

### Step 5: Finalize Row Echelon Form
Eliminate the last remaining entry under the third pivot:
* Operation: $R_4 \to R_4 - 4R_3$

$$\sim \begin{bmatrix} 
1 & 0 & 1 \\ 
0 & 1 & -1 \\ 
0 & 0 & 1 \\ 
0 & 0 & 0 
\end{bmatrix}$$

### Determining the Rank:
Count the number of non-zero rows remaining in this row echelon form. There are **3** non-zero rows. 
* Thus, the rank of matrix $A$ is **$3$**, written as **$\rho(A) = 3$**.

## Step-by-Step Worked Example 2: Matrix with Linearly Dependent Rows

Let's find the rank of a $4 \times 4$ matrix $B$:

$$B = \begin{bmatrix} 
0 & 1 & -3 & -1 \\ 
1 & 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 
3 & 1 & 0 & 2 \\ 
1 & 1 & -2 & 0 
\end{bmatrix}$$

### Reduction Steps:
1. **Swap rows** to put a non-zero leading entry at the top: $R_1 \leftrightarrow R_2$.
2. **Eliminate entries** in column 1: $R_3 \to R_3 - 3R_1$ and $R_4 \to R_4 - R_1$.
3. **Clear subsequent rows** using the second pivot: $R_3 \to R_3 - R_2$ and $R_4 \to R_4 - R_2$.

The resulting REF matrix becomes:
$$\sim \begin{bmatrix} 
1 & 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 
0 & 1 & -3 & -1 \\ 
0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 
0 & 0 & 0 & 0 
\end{bmatrix}$$

### Determining the Rank:
Notice that two entire rows have collapsed into zeros. Only **2** non-zero rows remain.
* Therefore, $\rho(B) = 2$.

## Summary Checklist for Rapid Revision

* [ ] **Definition:** The rank of a matrix equals the total count of non-zero rows in its **Row Echelon Form**.
* [ ] **Notation:** Represented as $\rho(A)$.
* [ ] **Constraint:** For an $m \times n$ matrix, $\rho(A) \le \min(m, n)$, meaning rank can never exceed the smaller dimension.
* [ ] **System Application:** A linear system $AX = B$ is consistent (has a solution) if and only if the rank of coefficient matrix $A$ equals the rank of the augmented matrix $[A|B]$.
