# Comprehensive Study Notes: Diagonalization of Matrices

Welcome to **Diagonalization**! In engineering and computer science (e.g., graphics rendering, population modeling, and Markov chains), computing high powers of a dense matrix $A^n$ can require thousands of multiplication operations. 

Diagonalization is a mathematical technique that simplifies a complicated square matrix into a **diagonal matrix**—where numbers only exist on the main diagonal, and everything else is zero. Computing $D^n$ for a diagonal matrix takes milliseconds because you simply raise each diagonal number to the power $n$!

---

## 1. High-Level Overview: Core Concepts

### What is Diagonalization?
A square matrix $A$ of size $n \times n$ is **diagonalizable** if it can be factored into three special matrices:
$$A = PDP^{-1} \quad \text{or equivalently} \quad P^{-1}AP = D$$

*   **$A$**: The original $n \times n$ matrix.
*   **$P$ (Modal Matrix):** A matrix whose **columns are the linearly independent eigenvectors** of $A$.
*   **$D$ (Spectral Matrix):** A **diagonal matrix** whose main diagonal entries are the corresponding **eigenvalues** of $A$.
*   **$P^{-1}$**: The inverse of matrix $P$.

---

### Key Theorems to Remember

1.  **The Diagonalization Theorem:** An $n \times n$ matrix $A$ is diagonalizable **if and only if** it has $n$ linearly independent eigenvectors.
2.  **Distinct Eigenvalues Condition:** If an $n \times n$ matrix $A$ has $n$ **distinct (all different)** eigenvalues, then $A$ is **guaranteed** to be diagonalizable.
3.  **Repeated Eigenvalues Condition:** If an eigenvalue repeats, the matrix can *still* be diagonalizable as long as the repeated eigenvalue produces enough linearly independent eigenvectors to total $n$ eigenvectors overall.

---

## 2. Step-by-Step Procedure to Diagonalize $A$

Follow this step-by-step algorithm to diagonalize any matrix on an exam:

1.  **Find Eigenvalues ($\lambda$):** Solve the characteristic equation $|A - \lambda I| = 0$.
2.  **Find Eigenvectors ($X_i$):** Solve $(A - \lambda I)X = 0$ for each eigenvalue to get $n$ linearly independent eigenvectors $X_1, X_2, \dots, X_n$.
3.  **Construct Modal Matrix $P$:** Combine eigenvectors as column vectors:
    $$P = \begin{bmatrix} X_1 & X_2 & \cdots & X_n \end{bmatrix}$$
4.  **Construct Spectral Matrix $D$:** Place eigenvalues on the diagonal matching the exact column order of $P$:
    $$D = \begin{bmatrix} \lambda_1 & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\ 0 & \lambda_2 & \cdots & 0 \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ 0 & 0 & \cdots & \lambda_n \end{bmatrix}$$
5.  **Calculate $P^{-1}$:** Find the inverse of $P$ using row operations $[P \mid I] \to [I \mid P^{-1}]$ or formulas.
6.  **Write Final Form:** Express $A = PDP^{-1}$ or verify $P^{-1}AP = D$.

---

## 3. Power and Inverse of a Matrix using Eigen-Decomposition

Once a matrix is in its diagonalized form $A = PDP^{-1}$, calculating powers and inverses becomes trivial.

### Calculating Powers ($A^n$)
Multiplying $A$ by itself $n$ times causes intermediate $P^{-1}P$ pairs to collapse into identity matrices $I$:
$$A^2 = (PDP^{-1})(PDP^{-1}) = PD(P^{-1}P)DP^{-1} = PD^2P^{-1}$$

By mathematical induction:
$$A^n = PD^nP^{-1}$$

Where $D^n$ is simply:
$$D^n = \begin{bmatrix} \lambda_1^n & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\ 0 & \lambda_2^n & \cdots & 0 \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ 0 & 0 & \cdots & \lambda_n^n \end{bmatrix}$$

---

### Calculating Inverses ($A^{-1}$)
Similarly, the inverse of matrix $A$ via eigen-decomposition is:
$$A^{-1} = PD^{-1}P^{-1}$$

Where $D^{-1}$ is formed by taking the reciprocal of each diagonal entry:
$$D^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix} \lambda_1^{-1} & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\ 0 & \lambda_2^{-1} & \cdots & 0 \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ 0 & 0 & \cdots & \lambda_n^{-1} \end{bmatrix}$$

## 4. Fully Worked Examples

### Example 1: 2x2 Matrix Diagonalization & Powers

**Problem:** Diagonalize the matrix $A = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -2 & -3 \end{bmatrix}$ and find $A^3$.

#### Step 1: Find Eigenvalues
$$|A - \lambda I| = \begin{vmatrix} -\lambda & 1 \\ -2 & -3-\lambda \end{vmatrix} = 0$$
$$(-\lambda)(-3 - \lambda) - (1)(-2) = 0 \implies \lambda^2 + 3\lambda + 2 = 0$$
$$(\lambda + 1)(\lambda + 2) = 0 \implies \lambda_1 = -1, \quad \lambda_2 = -2$$

#### Step 2: Find Eigenvectors

*   **For $\lambda_1 = -1$:**
    $$(A + I)X = 0 \implies \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ -2 & -2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$$
    Row operation $R_2 \to R_2 + 2R_1$ gives $x + y = 0 \implies y = -x$.
    Let $x = k$; then $X_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}$.

*   **For $\lambda_2 = -2$:**
    $$(A + 2I)X = 0 \implies \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ -2 & -1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$$
    Row operation $R_2 \to R_2 + R_1$ gives $2x + y = 0 \implies y = -2x$.
    Let $x = k$; then $X_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -2 \end{bmatrix}$.

#### Step 3: Construct $P$, $D$, and $P^{-1}$
*   **Modal Matrix $P$:** $P = \begin{bmatrix} X_1 & X_2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ -1 & -2 \end{bmatrix}$
*   **Spectral Matrix $D$:** $D = \begin{bmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & -2 \end{bmatrix}$
*   **Find $P^{-1}$:**
    Using Row Reduction on $[P \mid I]$:
    $$\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & \mid & 1 & 0 \\ -1 & -2 & \mid & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \xrightarrow{R_2 \to R_2 + R_1} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & \mid & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 & \mid & 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$
    $$\xrightarrow{R_2 \to -R_2} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & \mid & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & \mid & -1 & -1 \end{bmatrix} \xrightarrow{R_1 \to R_1 - R_2} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & \mid & 2 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & \mid & -1 & -1 \end{bmatrix}$$
    Thus, $P^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ -1 & -1 \end{bmatrix}$.

#### Step 4: Diagonalization Matrix Expression
$$A = PDP^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ -1 & -2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & -2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ -1 & -1 \end{bmatrix}$$

#### Step 5: Compute $A^3$
Using $A^3 = PD^3P^{-1}$:
$$D^3 = \begin{bmatrix} (-1)^3 & 0 \\ 0 & (-2)^3 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & -8 \end{bmatrix}$$
$$PD^3 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ -1 & -2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & -8 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -1 & -8 \\ 1 & 16 \end{bmatrix}$$
$$A^3 = (PD^3)P^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix} -1 & -8 \\ 1 & 16 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ -1 & -1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 6 & 7 \\ -14 & -15 \end{bmatrix}$$

### Example 2: 3x3 Matrix with Repeated Eigenvalues

**Problem:** Diagonalize $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 3 & 3 \\ -3 & -5 & -3 \\ 3 & 3 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$.

#### Step 1: Find Eigenvalues
Solving $|A - \lambda I| = 0$ yields:
$$-\lambda^3 - 3\lambda^2 + 4 = 0 \implies \lambda = 1, -2, -2$$

#### Step 2: Find Eigenvectors

*   **For Distinct Eigenvalue $\lambda = 1$:**
    $$(A - I)X = 0 \implies \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 3 & 3 \\ -3 & -6 & -3 \\ 3 & 3 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \\ x_3 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$$
    Row reduction to Echelon form gives equations:
    $$x_1 + x_2 = 0, \quad x_2 + x_3 = 0$$
    Setting free variable $x_2 = k$ gives $x_1 = -k$ and $x_3 = -k$.
    $$X_1 = \begin{bmatrix} -1 \\ 1 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}$$

*   **For Repeated Eigenvalue $\lambda = -2$:**
    $$(A + 2I)X = 0 \implies \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 3 & 3 \\ -3 & -3 & -3 \\ 3 & 3 & 3 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \\ x_3 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$$
    Row reducing yields only **one** independent row equation:
    $$x_1 + x_2 + x_3 = 0 \implies x_3 = -x_1 - x_2$$
    Because we have **two free variables**, we can generate **two linearly independent eigenvectors**:
    Let $x_1 = a$ and $x_2 = b$:
    $$X = \begin{bmatrix} a \\ b \\ -a - b \end{bmatrix} = a \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix} + b \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}$$
    Thus:
    $$X_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}, \quad X_3 = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}$$

#### Step 3: Construct $P$ and $D$
Since we obtained 3 linearly independent eigenvectors in total, $A$ is **diagonalizable**!

*   **Modal Matrix $P$:** $P = \begin{bmatrix} X_1 & X_2 & X_3 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -1 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 1 \\ -1 & -1 & -1 \end{bmatrix}$
*   **Spectral Matrix $D$:** $D = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & -2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & -2 \end{bmatrix}$

#### Step 4: Inverse $P^{-1}$ & Final Decomposition
By row reducing $[P \mid I] \to [I \mid P^{-1}]$, we get:
$$P^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix} -1 & -1 & -1 \\ 0 & -1 & -1 \\ 1 & 2 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

**Final Diagonalized Representation ($A = PDP^{-1}$):**
$$A = \begin{bmatrix} -1 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 1 \\ -1 & -1 & -1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & -2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & -2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} -1 & -1 & -1 \\ 0 & -1 & -1 \\ 1 & 2 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$
