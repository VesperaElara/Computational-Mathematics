# Solving and Analyzing Linear Systems: Worked Examples

---

## High-Level Overview

To determine whether a system of linear equations is consistent, has a unique solution, or has infinitely many solutions, we rely on **Rank Analysis of Augmented Matrices**. 

By using elementary row transformations, we reduce the augmented matrix $[A \mid B]$ to an **Echelon Form** (triangular form) and compare:
1. The rank of the coefficient matrix $\rho(A)$.
2. The rank of the full augmented matrix $\rho(A \mid B)$.
3. The total number of unknowns ($n$).

---

## 1. Testing Consistency (Numerical Examples)

### Example 1: Testing a $2 \times 2$ System
**Problem:** Test for consistency of the system:
$$x + 5y = 7, \quad -2x - 7y = -5$$

#### Solution:
1. Write the system as a matrix equation $AX = B$:
   $$\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 5 \\ -2 & -7 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 7 \\ -5 \end{bmatrix}$$
2. Form the augmented matrix $[A \mid B]$:
   $$[A \mid B] = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 5 & \mid & 7 \\ -2 & -7 & \mid & -5 \end{bmatrix}$$
3. Apply row operation $R_2 \to R_2 + 2R_1$:
   $$\sim \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 5 & \mid & 7 \\ 0 & 3 & \mid & 9 \end{bmatrix}$$
4. **Analysis:** Here, $\rho(A) = \rho(A \mid B) = 2$, which matches the number of unknowns ($n = 2$).
5. **Conclusion:** The system is **consistent and has a unique solution**.

---

### Example 2: Testing a $3 \times 3$ System
**Problem:** Test for consistency of the system:
$$x + y + z = 6, \quad x - y + 2z = 5, \quad 3x + y + z = 8$$

#### Solution:
1. Set up the augmented matrix $[A \mid B]$:
   $$[A \mid B] = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 & \mid & 6 \\ 1 & -1 & 2 & \mid & 5 \\ 3 & 1 & 1 & \mid & 8 \end{bmatrix}$$
2. Apply row transformations $R_2 \to R_2 - R_1$ and $R_3 \to R_3 - 3R_1$:
   $$\sim \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 & \mid & 6 \\ 0 & -2 & 1 & \mid & -1 \\ 0 & -2 & -2 & \mid & -10 \end{bmatrix}$$
3. Apply $R_3 \to R_3 - R_2$:
   $$\sim \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 & \mid & 6 \\ 0 & -2 & 1 & \mid & -1 \\ 0 & 0 & -3 & \mid & -9 \end{bmatrix}$$
4. **Analysis:** We get $\rho(A) = \rho(A \mid B) = 3$, which equals the number of unknowns ($n = 3$).
5. **Conclusion:** The system is **consistent and has a unique solution**.

## 2. Parameter-Based Consistency Conditions

### Example 3: Finding a Condition for Consistency
**Problem:** Find the condition under which the system is consistent:
$$3x + 4y + 5z = \alpha, \quad 4x + 5y + 6z = \beta, \quad 5x + 6y + 7z = \gamma$$

#### Solution:
1. Construct the augmented matrix $[A \mid B]$:
   $$[A \mid B] = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 4 & 5 & \mid & \alpha \\ 4 & 5 & 6 & \mid & \beta \\ 5 & 6 & 7 & \mid & \gamma \end{bmatrix}$$
2. Perform row operations to reduce the matrix:
   * $R_3 \to R_3 - R_2 \implies \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 4 & 5 & \mid & \alpha \\ 4 & 5 & 6 & \mid & \beta \\ 1 & 1 & 1 & \mid & \gamma - \beta \end{bmatrix}$
   * Swap and simplify to eliminate terms, eventually yielding:
     $$\sim \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 4 & 5 & \mid & \alpha \\ 0 & -1 & -2 & \mid & 3\beta - 4\alpha \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & \mid & \gamma - 2\beta + \alpha \end{bmatrix}$$
3. **Analysis:** For the system to be consistent, the rank of $A$ must equal the rank of $[A \mid B]$. Since the left side has a zero row, $\rho(A) = 2$. Therefore, the augmented part must also evaluate to zero to prevent rank mismatch.
4. **Conclusion:** The system is consistent only if **$\alpha - 2\beta + \gamma = 0$**.

### Example 4: Investigating Parameters for Unique, None, or Infinite Solutions
**Problem:** Investigate the values of $\lambda$ and $\mu$ so that the system has:
1. A unique solution
2. No solution
3. An infinite number of solutions

Equations: 
$$2x + 3y + 5z = 9, \quad 7x + 3y - 2z = 8, \quad 2x + 3y + \lambda z = \mu$$

#### Solution:
1. Write the augmented matrix and reduce it using elementary row operations:
   $$[A \mid B] = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 3 & 5 & \mid & 9 \\ 7 & 3 & -2 & \mid & 8 \\ 2 & 3 & \lambda & \mid & \mu \end{bmatrix}$$
2. After applying row reductions ($R_1 \to R_1/2$, followed by elimination), the matrix reaches the form:
   $$\sim \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 3/2 & 5/2 & \mid & 9/2 \\ 0 & -15/2 & -39/2 & \mid & -47/2 \\ 0 & 0 & \lambda - 5 & \mid & \mu - 9 \end{bmatrix}$$

3. **Evaluating the Cases:**
   * **Unique Solution:** Requires $\rho(A) = \rho(A \mid B) = 3$. This happens when $\lambda - 5 \neq 0$, meaning **$\lambda \neq 5$ and $\mu$ can be any real number**.
   * **No Solution:** Requires $\rho(A) \neq \rho(A \mid B)$. This occurs if $\lambda = 5$ while $\mu \neq 9$ (creating a row like $[0 \ 0 \ 0 \mid \text{non-zero}]$).
   * **Infinite Solutions:** Requires $\rho(A) = \rho(A \mid B) < 3$. This occurs when **$\lambda = 5$ and $\mu = 9$** simultaneously, reducing the rank to 2 with 3 unknowns.

## 3. Homogeneous Systems of Linear Equations

When $B = 0$ ($AX = 0$):
* **Trivial Solution:** If $\rho(A) = n$ (number of unknowns), the system has only the zero solution ($x = y = z = 0$).
* **Infinite Solutions:** If $\rho(A) < n$, the system has $n - r$ linearly independent solutions, resulting in an **infinite number of non-zero solutions**.

### Example 5: Solving a Homogeneous System
**Problem:** Solve the system:
$$x + 2y + 3z = 0, \quad 3x + 4y + 4z = 0, \quad 7x + 10y + 12z = 0$$

#### Solution:
1. Write the coefficient matrix $A$:
   $$A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 3 & 4 & 4 \\ 7 & 10 & 12 \end{bmatrix}$$
2. Apply row transformations ($R_2 \to R_2 - 3R_1$, $R_3 \to R_3 - 7R_1$):
   $$\sim \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 0 & -2 & -5 \\ 0 & -4 & -9 \end{bmatrix}$$
3. Apply $R_3 \to R_3 - 2R_2$:
   $$\sim \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 0 & -2 & -5 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$
4. **Analysis:** The rank of the coefficient matrix is $\rho(A) = 3$, which matches the number of unknowns ($n = 3$).
5. **Conclusion:** The system has only the **trivial zero solution**: $x = 0, y = 0, z = 0$.
